# The STAR Method

**Situation** — Context: what team, what system, what was at stake. 1-2 sentences, enough to
orient a stranger, no more.

**Task** — Your specific responsibility or the specific problem you owned. Distinguish "the team
did X" from "I specifically did Y" — interviewers are evaluating you, not your team.

**Action** — What *you* actually did, step by step. This is the bulk of the answer. Be concrete:
name the technology, the decision you made, the tradeoff you considered and rejected.

**Result** — Quantify if at all possible (time saved, incidents prevented, latency reduced,
$ saved, % improvement). If you can't quantify, describe the qualitative outcome and what
changed afterward (a new standard, a process adopted elsewhere, a promotion/recognition).

## Common failure modes to avoid
- **"We" language throughout** — interviewers will explicitly ask "what did *you* do" if your
  answer stays in "we." Pre-empt it by using "I" for your actions, "we" only for team context.
- **No real conflict/difficulty** — a story where nothing went wrong is forgettable. The
  strongest stories have a real obstacle (technical, interpersonal, or a tight deadline) and show
  how you handled it.
- **Rambling Situation, thin Action** — spend 70% of your time on Action. If you're 90 seconds in
  and still on Situation, cut it short.
- **No Result** — always land the plane. Even "the migration shipped on time and we haven't had
  an incident since" is a result.

## Standard question categories to prep for (senior-level bar)
- Tell me about a time you disagreed with a technical decision.
- Tell me about a time you had to deliver under a tight deadline with incomplete information.
- Tell me about a time something failed in production — what happened and what did you learn.
- Tell me about a time you had to influence people outside your direct reporting line
  (cross-team coordination — very relevant to migration work).
- Tell me about a time you mentored someone or raised the bar on your team.
- Tell me about a time you had to say no to a request, or push back on scope.
- Tell me about a time you identified a risk nobody else had noticed.

## Delivery tips
- Have 5-6 core stories that each can flex to answer 2-3 different question categories, rather
  than memorizing one story per possible question — you can't predict the exact phrasing.
- Practice out loud, not just in your head — pacing and "um"-density only show up when spoken.
- It's fine to pause for 3-5 seconds to pick the right story before answering. Silence reads
  better than a rambling wrong-story answer.
